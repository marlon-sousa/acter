# B4.3 — a teardown read that races the close

Roadmap entry 22.3. Measured 2026-08-23, before anything was proposed, because the entry
named three candidates with different consequences and said whose defect it is had to be
decided before anything was written.

`a_shell_that_exits_ends_the_session_by_closing_the_channel` in `local_pty.rs` runs
`cmd /C echo done` and asserts that the **next** thing off the read channel is the channel
closing. Sometimes one more read arrives first: `ESC [ ? 9 0 0 1 l` followed by
`ESC [ ? 1 0 0 4 l`, ConPTY turning win32-input-mode and focus-event reporting back off as
it tears the pseudoconsole down.

## The measurement, which decides it

### It is a coin flip, not a certainty

The entry's 2026-08-23 paragraph recorded that the flake had become deterministic here —
three runs, one of them serial as CI runs it, all red. **That is not reproducible.** Three
ways of running it on `2f9467d`:

- the test alone, serial, five runs: **one** red;
- the whole real-shell suite, serial with `--test-threads=1`, three runs: **two** red;
- a probe spawning `cmd /C echo done` thirty times in one process: **fourteen** red.

So it is a race at roughly even odds on this machine, and yesterday's three reds were a run
of the same coin. That matters because the entry drew an inference from the determinism —
that a race would not land identically five times, so the test's expectation was the likely
culprit rather than the transport. The conclusion survives; the reasoning behind it does
not, and is replaced by the two measurements below.

### The bytes carry no meaning, and that is the half that matters

When the read arrives it is always the same sixteen bytes, 0.3 to 5 milliseconds after the
read carrying `done`. Never two reads, never anything else.

Fed to a real `AlacrittyEngine`, those bytes produce **no `TerminalItem` at all** — not an
empty line, nothing — in every one of the fourteen runs that saw them. Nothing is rendered,
nothing reaches a block, nothing is spoken. The entry filed this as unmeasured and as the
half that matters to a user rather than to a test: an escape sequence read aloud at the end
of every session would be a real defect that the flake was only the symptom of. It is not
happening.

### Real output is never truncated by the teardown

The entry's third candidate is that the watcher thread drops the master while the reader can
still be mid-drain, making the race the transport's. If that were true the epilogue would be
the harmless symptom of a mechanism that can also lose **output**, which would be a genuine
and much worse bug.

Probed directly: `cmd /C for /L %i in (1,1,2000) do @echo line-%i` — two thousand lines and
then an immediate exit, twenty runs. Every line arrived in every run, and the total was
**20970 bytes every single time**, byte-identical across all twenty. The teardown never cuts
off output the shell actually produced.

So what races is only ConPTY's mode-reset epilogue, addressed to a terminal that is being
destroyed, meaning nothing in either outcome. **That is the entry's first candidate: the
test is asserting the outcome of a race, and "the next thing is the close" is the wrong
assertion.**

### What CI is doing is not explained

The real-shell job is green on every recent run of main. A race at even odds landing green
every time on the runner is odd, and this spec does not claim to know why — only that the
runner's teardown timing lands the other way from this machine's. Draining makes the suite
correct on both regardless, which is the reason not to spend a PR chasing it.

## Decision 1 — the test drains to the close

`a_shell_that_exits_ends_the_session_by_closing_the_channel` reads until the channel closes
rather than asserting on the next read, which is exactly what
`writing_to_a_shell_that_exited_says_the_session_ended` beside it already does.

What the `Transport` port models is that a session has one ending and it is the channel
closing (spec B4, decision 3). That is what the test asserts, within `PATIENCE`. A
pseudoconsole is entitled to say something on its way down, and how many reads that takes is
not a property this suite has any business pinning.

## Decision 2 — the drain asserts the epilogue is meaningless

Everything read after `done` is fed through the engine and the items are collected, and the
test asserts there are none.

Draining and discarding would make the test pass; it would also throw away the finding. What
is pinned instead is the fact the measurement established — **a teardown epilogue produces
nothing a user could hear** — so that if ConPTY ever starts emitting something with text in
it, this suite says so rather than silently draining it away. The assertion message prints
the bytes and the items, because a failure here is a change in what Windows does and the
next person needs to see it.

`Shell::answer` already computes those items and drops them on the floor; it now returns
them. Existing callers are call statements and are unaffected.

## Rejected

**Skipping or quarantining the test.** It has never been skipped, and the entry notes the
`real-shell (Windows)` job has presumably been intermittently red for this all along. The
assertion was wrong; the test is not.

**Fixing it in `local.rs` by draining before dropping the master.** The entry's third
candidate, and it was on the table until the two-thousand-line probe came back. There is
nothing to drain that anyone wants: output is never lost, and the bytes that do race carry
no meaning. Ordering the teardown to guarantee delivery of a mode reset to a terminal being
destroyed would be machinery bought for no one.

**Asserting the sixteen bytes when they appear.** Tempting, since they are byte-identical
whenever they arrive. It would pin a ConPTY implementation detail this product does not
depend on, and it would fail on the runner, where they do not arrive at all.

**Widening the drain to the other real-shell tests.** They read until they see what they are
looking for and never assert on the shape of the ending, so none of them is exposed to this.

## Acceptance criteria

1. `a_shell_that_exits_ends_the_session_by_closing_the_channel` passes repeatedly — twenty
   consecutive serial runs, against a race previously landing red at roughly even odds.
2. It still fails if the session never ends: the close is asserted within `PATIENCE` rather
   than waited for indefinitely.
3. Bytes arriving between the last output and the close are fed through the engine, and the
   test fails if any of them produces a `TerminalItem`.
4. The whole real-shell suite passes serially, as CI runs it.
5. The workspace suite still passes, with nothing skipped or quarantined.

## Definition of done

The real-shell suite is green on repeated serial runs, the workspace suite is green, and the
roadmap entry is Done with the corrected measurement recorded.
