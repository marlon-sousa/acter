# Spec: 13.3 — the far end a connection reached is spoken, every time

Roadmap entries **13.3** (lane 1) and **23.13** (lane 2), agreed in conversation 2026-08-30.
They are one defect filed twice from two different NVDA passes, so one PR fixes both and
23.13 closes by reference to this one.

## Why now

Connecting announces what you are now typing into — "connected to Command Prompt",
"connected to WSL: Ubuntu, bash", "connected to Command Prompt, started although nothing has
signed it". Six times across five NVDA passes it was not spoken at all: the listener got the
terminal window, the edit field and the shell's own prompt, and never what they had connected
to. Two of B9.5's four connections spoke and two did not, which is the shape of it — nothing
was broken every time.

It costs more than a missed utterance since B5.5. For a far end Acter cannot integrate, the
named sentence is the *only* one said — the generic `IntegrationUnavailable` is deliberately
suppressed when the note already carried it — so a listener on such a distribution heard
neither, where before B5.5 they at least heard the generic sentence five seconds later.

## What was measured

All of it on 2026-08-30, NVDA 2026.1.1, `user` persona, driven through the screen-readers
bridge against real far ends, silent capture except where live is named.

**Both suspicions the entry was filed with are wrong.** The text reaches `#announcer`
correctly every time — in the document, not inert, nothing over it — and stays about two
seconds, so the region is not going away and `CLEAR_AFTER_MS` does not eat it. And the reader
is not busy: it emits other utterances 60 to 200 ms later, and speaks the shell prompt
appended to that same region half a second afterwards, 21 to 27 ms after that insertion.

**What is lost is the first live-region change carrying text after the region's document
returns to the accessibility tree.** A modal makes the rest of the document inert; when it
closes, the region comes back with no earlier state for the reader to compare a change
against, and the first change is discarded. Two probes fix that: a marker inserted into
`#announcer` one millisecond after the swallowed sentence *was* spoken, so this is not "wait
longer"; and letting a harmless **empty** node go first failed on the real string in the real
order, because an empty node is not a text change at all.

**Holding the dialogs open until the words had been taken does not work.** A margin was
bisected first — fifteen trials putting one line in the Connect dialog's live region and
closing it after a set wait: heard in 2 of 4 at zero margin, and 11 of 11 from 17 ms upwards.
Built on that, the announcer learned to say when it had been drained and the Connect dialog
waited 100 ms before closing anything. Driven against the real application it was heard in
**0 of 3**, because it made the sentence land deterministically in the *connecting dialog's*
region, which is then taken away — and worse, that region kept the text, so the next
connection was greeted with the previous far end's sentence: "connecting to Command Prompt",
then "connected to WSL: Ubuntu, bash". This is why the fix below is an order and a baseline
rather than a wait.

**Saying it after both dialogs close is most of the answer**: 4 of 5 heard, the one failure
being the first connection of a freshly launched window.

**And giving the reader something to lose first is the rest.** With a wordless change written
into the region immediately before the sentence, it was heard in **10 of 10** — three first
connections of freshly launched windows, six later ones, and a WSL: Ubuntu through the setup
dialog. The marker itself must carry text and say nothing, and both halves are measured: a
full stop worked and was **audible** — in live capture the synthesizer said "ponto" before
every connection — while a zero-width space was heard in 5 of 5 and the listener at the
keyboard reported hearing nothing at all before it.

## Design decisions this spec makes

**1. The sentence is said after the dialogs close, not while they are open.** `connectTo` no
longer announces; `AppController.announceConnection` does, and `ConnectDialog.chosen` calls it
after `dialog.close()`. The words stay pinned in the controller with every other announced
string — what moved is the moment, and the moment belongs to whoever knows the dialogs are out
of the way. It is also the honest order: the sentence describes the window the listener is now
in, not the dialog they have left.

**2. `AnnouncerView.documentReturned()` re-establishes the baseline.** It queues one wordless
change, so the announcement behind it is not the first one after the region came back. It goes
through the ordinary queue, which is what makes it work: the drain spacing keeps it out of the
next announcement's mutation batch, so the reader sees two changes rather than one.

**3. The marker is a zero-width space** (`SILENT_MARKER` in `announcer.ts`), with the full
stop's audibility and the empty node's failure written into its comment. Both dead ends are
recorded there so neither is revived.

**4. Each region is cleared on its own countdown.** The announcer kept one clear timer, so a
drain into the document's region cancelled the countdown belonging to a dialog's and the
dialog kept its last words indefinitely. That is what made the stale sentence available to be
spoken at the next connection. `clearTimers` is now a map keyed by region.

**5. The settle gate is not kept.** `settled()` and its measured margin are removed rather
than left in beside the real fix: they were built on the bisection, they were heard in 0 of 3
against the real application, and keeping them would cost every connection a wait for
something no measurement supports.

## Tests

- The marker is put into the region before the next announcement, carries text (the empty
  node's lesson), says nothing a listener can hear, and takes one ordinary drain.
- A region is cleared on its own countdown, so a dialog does not keep its last words when
  something is said into the document afterwards.
- The Connect dialog closes, says the document is back, and only then names the far end — in
  that order — and does none of it when the attempt is refused.
- `connectTo` announces nothing; `announceConnection` announces once, and answers false when
  there is no connection to name.

## Acceptance criteria

- The sentence naming the far end is spoken on the first connection of a freshly launched
  window, which is the case every one of the six observations shared.
- Nothing else about connecting changes: same words, same failure behaviour, focus still lands
  in the edit field on success and on the kind list on failure.
- `npm run typecheck` and the vitest suite pass.

## Manual accessibility checklist (PR body)

Agent-observed through the screen-readers bridge, NVDA 2026.1.1, `user` persona, against the
landed build, with what was spoken recorded inline. The audibility of the marker is
human-verified, because whether a symbol is voiced depends on a punctuation setting no capture
can hear for the listener.

## Out of scope

- The separate-region, polite-versus-assertive and cross-region-order questions DESIGN still
  holds open. This spec adds one placement-and-timing rule; it does not build the announcement
  channel model.
- `STARTUP_HOLD_MS`, which is a starting point rather than a measurement and stays one.
- Any other caller that closes a dialog onto an announcement. Rule 13 states the obligation;
  the Connect dialogs are the only pair that does it today.
