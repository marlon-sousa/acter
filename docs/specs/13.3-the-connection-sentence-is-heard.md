# Spec: 13.3 — the far end a connection reached is spoken, every time

Roadmap entries **13.3** (lane 1) and **23.13** (lane 2), agreed in conversation 2026-08-30.
They are one defect filed twice from two different NVDA passes, so one PR fixes both and
23.13 closes by reference to this one.

## Why now

Connecting announces what you are now typing into — "connected to Command Prompt",
"connected to WSL: Ubuntu, bash", "connected to Command Prompt, started although nothing has
signed it". Six times across five NVDA passes it was not spoken at all: the listener got the
terminal window, the edit field and the shell's own prompt, and never what they had connected
to. Two of B9.5's four connections spoke and two did not, which is the shape of it — nothing
is broken every time.

It costs more than a missed utterance since B5.5. For a far end Acter cannot integrate, the
named sentence is the *only* one said — the generic `IntegrationUnavailable` is deliberately
suppressed when the note already carried it — so a listener on such a distribution hears
neither, where before B5.5 they at least heard the generic sentence five seconds later.

## What was measured, and what it rules out

NVDA 2026.1.1, silent capture, `user` persona, driven through the screen-readers bridge
against a real Command Prompt far end, with the debug binary's embedded WebDriver recording
the DOM on the same wall clock, 2026-08-30.

**Both suspicions 13.3 was filed with are wrong.**

- The text reaches `#announcer` correctly every time — in the document, not inert, no modal
  over it, no hidden ancestor — and stays about two seconds. The region is not going away and
  `CLEAR_AFTER_MS` does not eat it.
- The reader is not busy. It emits other utterances 60 to 200 ms later, and it speaks the
  shell prompt appended to that same region half a second afterwards, 21 to 27 ms after that
  insertion.

**What is lost is the first live-region change carrying text after the region returns to the
accessibility tree.** Acter closes both modal dialogs and drains the announcement in the same
millisecond, so the sentence arrives exactly as the document is re-admitted and the reader has
no earlier state to compare it against.

Two probes established that, and both close a door:

- A marker inserted into `#announcer` one millisecond after the swallowed sentence *was*
  spoken; the sentence was not. So this is not "wait longer" in any simple sense.
- Letting a harmless **empty** node go first, to re-establish the baseline, was tried on the
  real string in the real order and failed. An empty node is not a text change. This idea is
  recorded here so it is not revived.

**The margin was then bisected.** Each trial opened the Connect dialog for real, put one line
in its live region, waited a set margin, then closed the dialog. Fifteen trials with speech
capture: at zero margin (1 to 2 ms actual) the line was heard in 2 of 4 trials; from 17 ms
upwards it was heard in 11 of 11, across 17, 30, 47, 66, 123 and 262 ms plus a three-second
control.

**And the risk this fix carries was measured and cleared.** A marker put into the connecting
dialog's live region 90 ms before that dialog closed was spoken 7 ms *after* the region left
the accessibility tree. Removing a region does not retract what the reader has already taken.

## Design decisions this spec makes

**1. The announcer can be asked whether it still owes anything.** `AnnouncerView` gains
`settled(): Promise<void>`, which resolves when the queue is empty, no drain is pending, and
the last drained announcement has had its margin. It is deliberately not called `spoken`: what
it reports is the reader having *taken* the words into its own speech queue, which the 90 ms
probe shows happens well before the utterance.

The promise describes the queue when it resolves rather than when it was asked for, so an
announcement arriving while a caller waits is waited for too. Otherwise a progress sentence
landing mid-wait would drain into a region that had just left the tree — this defect again,
with different words.

**2. Whoever takes a live region away waits for it first.** `ConnectDialog.chosen` awaits
`announcer.settled()` before `connecting.hide()` and `dialog.close()`. The announcement is the
controller's and the teardown is the dialog's, and neither learns about the other: the dialog
already holds the announcer for its own two announcements, so this adds no dependency and no
new wiring.

The whole queue rather than the one sentence, for the reason in decision 1. Both paths wait —
a refusal has whatever the connection said on its way to failing sitting in the same region.

**3. The margin is 100 ms**, `SPOKEN_MARGIN_MS` in `announcer.ts`, beside the two constants
that already carry their own measurements. The bisection puts the threshold below 17 ms, so
this is about six times the highest it can be; it is generous because it is cheap — paid once
per connection, at the end of a wait measured in seconds. Parameterised through the
constructor exactly as `startupHold` is, so vitest can pass zero and go on asserting the
timing rules measured against a real reader.

**4. No cap on the wait.** A bound would reintroduce the defect for whatever exceeded it. The
queue here is bounded by the far end's own progress sentences at one drain each; if that ever
holds the dialog too long, the answer is fewer sentences, not a cap.

**5. `busy(false)` moves after the wait.** The attempt is over when what it said has been
taken, not when it answered — so the Connect dialog underneath keeps saying its controls are
unavailable for the margin, which is true for the margin.

**6. It becomes a dialog rule.** ARCHITECTURE's dialog list gains rule 13: a dialog is not
closed while the announcer still owes it words. Every rule in that list is a fix for something
that shipped, and this is the second surface it could happen on — any future dialog that
announces and then closes has the same defect waiting in it.

Nothing in `app.ts` changes: it announces exactly as it did.

## Tests

- `settled` is settled at once when nothing was ever announced.
- It waits for an announcement that has not drained yet, and for the margin after it drains —
  asserted at the margin boundary in both directions.
- It waits out the startup hold as well.
- It waits for an announcement that arrives while it is being waited on.
- Every caller waiting is released together.
- The Connect dialog keeps both dialogs open, and its controls unavailable, until the
  announcer settles; it closes them once it has; a refusal waits the same way before putting
  the listener back on the list.

## Acceptance criteria

- The sentence naming the far end is spoken on the first connection of a freshly launched
  window, which is the case every one of the six observations shared.
- Nothing else about connecting changes: same words, same order, same failure behaviour,
  focus still lands in the edit field on success and on the kind list on failure.
- `npm run typecheck` and the vitest suite pass.

## Manual accessibility checklist (PR body)

Agent-observable through the screen-readers bridge (NVDA 2026.1.1, silent capture, `user`
persona), and recorded inline with what was spoken:

- Connect to Command Prompt as the first connection of a freshly launched window, four times
  over, and hear "connected to Command Prompt" every time. Four because the defect it replaces
  was 2 in 4.
- Connect to a cold WSL distribution and hear the starting sentence, then the connection
  sentence naming the distribution and its shell, then the prompt.
- Connect a second time in the same window and hear the new far end named.
- A connection that cannot be started still says why, and focus is on the kind list.

## Out of scope

- The separate-region, polite-versus-assertive and cross-region-order questions DESIGN still
  holds open.
- `STARTUP_HOLD_MS`, which is a starting point rather than a measurement and stays one.
- Any other caller that takes a region away. Rule 13 states the obligation; the Connect
  dialogs are the only pair that closes on an announcement today.
